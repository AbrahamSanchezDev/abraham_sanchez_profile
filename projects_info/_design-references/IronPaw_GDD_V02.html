01

Game Overview

Concept Statement

Iron Paws is a gritty 3D tower defense / action game set on an futuristic industrial invaded world. The player pilots an agile combat mech — driven by one of three anthropomorphic animal pilots — and must defend one of the last remaining strongholds against relentless waves of bug-like alien creatures. Combat, base management, resource gathering, and rescue missions interweave into a tense, evolving gameplay loop.

Pillars

Feel powerful, stay challenged. The mech grows with the player through power-ups, but the alien threat always escalates to match.

Every second matters. Downtime between waves is active — collect, rescue, upgrade, explore.

Personal stakes. This is the pilot's home world. The base holds survivors. Failure is meaningful.

Target Audience

Fans of action-focused tower defense hybrids (e.g. Sanctum, Orcs Must Die). Players who enjoy mech / action games with satisfying combat feedback. PC gamers who appreciate sci-fi aesthetics with strong character personality.

Ages 16+ Action fans Tower defense fans Sci-fi lovers
02

World & Setting

"They didn't come with ships. They came with rocks. And when the rocks hit the ground, chaos emerged."

The World

The game takes place on the pilot's home planet — a once-advanced civilization now in the final stages of collapse. The alien invasion did not arrive as a fleet but as a meteor shower, each meteorite a biological delivery system carrying swarms of bug-like creatures. Years after the initial impact, the planet's cities lie in ruin.

Alongside the creatures came their growth: a spreading alien jungle — bioluminescent, aggressive, and deeply unsettling. It is not native flora. It is the invasion made permanent. It creeps through broken concrete, climbs rusted towers, and pulses with eerie light at night. The further the player ventures from the stronghold, the denser the alien growth becomes.

Visual Setting

Ruined futuristic city overtaken by alien jungle. Crumbling skyscrapers wrapped in glowing vines. Shattered highways home to alien root systems. Flooded plazas lit by bioluminescent growths. Cold dead metal versus living, pulsing alien color.

Broken concrete Exposed wiring Alien flora Bioluminescence Rusted steel

The Stronghold

The player's base is one of the last functional strongholds on the planet. Survivors live here. Wounded colleagues return here. It must not fall. The stronghold's condition and defenses are central to gameplay — it is as much a character as the pilot.

The Alien Growth as Threat

The alien jungle is not passive scenery. It acts as a living threat indicator: denser overgrowth signals deeper enemy territory and higher danger. Flora can spawn smaller enemies, block player paths, and obstruct base perimeters. Rare alien plants also drop valuable resources — making harvesting a risk-reward decision.

Pilots

The player can customize the name and color of the mech, and select one of three anthropomorphic animal pilots before each run. Each pilot is a distinct character with their own personality, visual identity, and mech color scheme. The mech chassis is identical across all pilots — only the color and pilot character differ. Pilot choice is cosmetic and narrative; it does not affect starting stats.

Design Notes

Pilots appear in cockpit view / HUD portraits during gameplay and in cutscenes. Voice lines are unique per pilot. Future iterations may explore minor stat differences per pilot, but initial scope keeps them cosmetically distinct only.

04

The Mech

Base Design

The mech is a medium-class combat unit — not a lumbering tank, not a fragile scout. It is agile and responsive, built to feel like an extension of the player's intention. Movement is fluid. Attacks have weight and feedback. The pilot feels in control at all times.

The base mech is shared across all pilots. Pilot selection changes its color scheme only. As the player collects power-ups during a run, the mech visually evolves — new weapon modules attach, panels shift, silhouette changes. Progression is visible.

Feel & Movement

Movement: fast, directional, responsive. No sluggishness. The mech can dash, reposition, and engage at any range. Players should feel like a predator, not a turret.

Agile Responsive Not heavy Not slow

Progression

Power-ups add new weapons and mechanics to the mech mid-run. Visual changes reflect each upgrade. Players build a personalized loadout each session through the order and type of power-ups they collect.

05

Combat System

Combat is the primary expression of player skill. The attack roster covers short, mid, and long range, with crowd-control and precision options. The full set unlocks progressively via power-ups — players start with the core three and grow from there.

🔫
Standard Shot
Default
Baseline ranged attack. Fast, reliable, mid-range. The bread and butter of every engagement. Always available.
⚡
Dash / Ram
Default
A high-speed charge that doubles as both mobility and melee attack. Close the gap or scatter a cluster of enemies. Directional.
💥
Smash
Default
Ground-pound or melee slam. High damage in a small radius. Excellent for crowd control when enemies swarm close. Has recovery time.
🌀
Energy Emission
Power-Up
A burst of energy radiating outward from the mech in all directions. Ideal against swarms closing in. Area-of-effect. Short cooldown.
🚀
Rocket Shot
Power-Up
Long-range heavy projectile. Locks onto distant or high-priority targets. High damage, slow reload. Ideal for intercepting incoming meteorites.
🛸
Sky Laser
Power-Up
Player marks a target location. A precision laser strikes from above. Satisfying and strategic. Useful for dense clusters or priority targets.

Combat Design Notes

Default attacks ensure the player is never helpless. Power-up attacks expand the toolkit without replacing fundamentals. The combination of short (Smash, Energy Emission), mid (Shot, Dash), and long range (Rocket, Sky Laser) means the player always has a contextually appropriate response to any threat. Combat should feel deliberate and rewarding, not button-mashy.

06

Enemies

Arrival: The Meteorite System

Enemies do not spawn from off-screen edges — they arrive via meteorites that crash-land into the environment. Each meteorite impact is a dynamic event: the player can see incoming trajectories, choose to intercept the meteorite mid-flight with ranged attacks, or brace for the swarm that emerges on impact. This system creates immediate moment-to-moment decisions and makes every wave feel like an event, not just a timer.

Base Enemy: Bug-Alien Swarm

The core enemy type is an insectoid alien creature — fast-moving, numerous, and threatening through sheer volume. Individually weak, collectively dangerous. Swarm behavior is the primary threat pattern.

Fast Numerous Swarm AI Insectoid

Flora-Spawned Enemies

The alien jungle is not just environmental dressing. Certain alien plant structures can spawn smaller creatures, making overgrown areas actively hostile. Destroying flora yields resources but may trigger an immediate local threat response.

Enemy Escalation

Each level increases swarm size, meteorite frequency, and enemy variety. Later levels should introduce elite or specialized enemy types — armored variants, fast runners, ranged attackers — to force players to use their full attack toolkit. Enemy types and escalation curves are TBD and will be developed during production.

07

Core Gameplay Loop

1
Meteorite Incoming
Player spots incoming meteorites on radar / visually in the sky. Decision point: intercept with long-range attacks, or prepare defenses for impact swarm. Timing and positioning matter.
2
Wave Combat
Swarms emerge from impact craters. Player engages using the full mech combat system — dash, smash, shoot, and unleash power-up abilities. Swarms target both the player and the base.
3
Active Downtime
Between waves, the player is not idle. They collect resources from the environment (including from alien flora), explore nearby areas, and prepare the base. Time pressure is implicit — the next wave is always coming.
4
Mission Layer
During active or downtime phases, mission objectives may trigger — most notably, finding a wounded colleague and carrying them back to base. Carrying limits mech speed, creating deliberate vulnerability.
5
Base Upgrade
Collected resources are spent on base defense systems — turrets, barriers, reinforced walls. These upgrades persist through the wave and reduce pressure on the player during combat.
6
Power-Up Acquisition
Power-ups are awarded through combat performance, mission completion, or found in the environment. Each power-up adds a new attack or mechanic to the mech. The mech visually evolves with each addition.
08

Base Defense System

Overview

The stronghold is a living part of the game — not just a health bar to protect. Players spend collected resources to upgrade and expand its defensive capabilities between waves. These upgrades reduce the load on the player during combat, allowing a balance between active mech gameplay and passive defense.

Defense Upgrade Examples

The following are initial concepts. Full design TBD during production:

Auto-turrets Reinforced walls Energy barriers Watchtowers Repair stations Floodlights

Resources

Resources are collected from the environment during downtime — dropped by defeated enemies, harvested from alien flora (with risk), or found in ruins. Alien flora harvesting yields rare, high-value materials but may trigger local enemy spawn. Risk vs. Reward.

Progression: Hybrid Persistence

Each level starts with the base reset — the stronghold does not carry over its upgrades. However, the player retains a small meaningful unlock between levels: a passive bonus, a weapon unlock, a permanent ability, or a narrative/story element. This creates cumulative progress without requiring full meta-progression balance — keeping scope manageable for a team of three while still rewarding continued play.

09

Missions & Objectives

Levels are structured around a primary objective (survive all waves, defend the base) with optional and triggered secondary missions layered throughout. Missions give purpose to downtime and deepen the narrative stakes.

Mission Type Description Gameplay Impact
Survive the Wave Defeat all enemies from a meteorite impact. Primary objective per wave. Core combat loop. Failure = base damage.
Rescue Colleague Locate a wounded colleague in the field and carry them back to the stronghold. Limits mech speed while carrying. Creates vulnerability. Story payoff on success.
Intercept Meteorite Destroy an incoming meteorite before it impacts, preventing a swarm. Prevents a full wave. Requires long-range attacks and fast repositioning.
Resource Run Collect a target amount of resources before the next wave hits. Drives exploration. Alien flora risk-reward. Time pressure.
Destroy Flora Node Eliminate a high-density alien growth structure spawning enemies. Stops passive enemy generation. High local risk on engagement.
10

Progression & Structure

Level Structure

The game is level-based with a clear start and end per mission. Each level has a defined set of waves, a narrative beat, and at least one rescue/mission objective. Levels escalate in enemy density, meteorite frequency, and environmental complexity. A campaign structure (overworld map or linear chapter select) will connect levels — design TBD.

Within a Run

Mech evolves through power-ups collected during the level. Base upgrades built from resources. No carryover of these between levels.

Between Runs (Persistence)

One meaningful unlock persists per completed level — a passive stat bonus, a new starting weapon option, or a narrative element. Keeps long-term engagement without full meta-progression complexity.

Power-Up Design Principles

Power-ups should feel immediately impactful. Each one visibly changes the mech and opens a new gameplay option. The order in which power-ups are found should create emergent build variety — no two runs feel identical. Power-ups are never passive stat boosts; they always add a new attack or mechanic.

Tone & Art Direction

Tone

Gritty sci-fi. The world has already lost. What remains is the will to hold on. The tone is serious but not hopeless — the pilots have personality and dark humor, but the stakes are real. There is no comic relief enemy. The alien threat is genuinely menacing.

Think: grounded military sci-fi meets character-driven animation aesthetic. The anthropomorphic pilots give the game personality and approachability without undermining the weight of the setting.

Visual Direction

Dark palette Bioluminescent accents Industrial decay Alien overgrowth Neon warning signs

Cold, broken metal contrasted with warm, pulsing alien color. The alien jungle should feel like it's breathing. Mech design is functional and worn — no pristine chrome.

Audio Direction

Heavy mechanical sound design for the mech. Organic, chittering, unsettling sounds for alien enemies. Score: tense industrial / electronic with moments of quiet dread between waves. Pilot voice lines: character-specific, sparse, impactful.
